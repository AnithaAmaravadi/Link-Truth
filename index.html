Red flags every human should know

// TRAIN YOUR EYES

01

Read the domain, not the pretty text.

In paypal.com.secure-alert.xyz the real site is secure-alert.xyz — everything before it is decoration. The true domain is always the part just before the first single slash.

02

Urgency is a weapon.

"Verify within 24 hours or your account will be locked" is pressure, not policy. Banks and services never threaten you into clicking links from messages.

03

Short links are sealed envelopes.

bit.ly, tinyurl.com, t.co hide the destination completely. Expand them first — never open a shortened link from a stranger.

04

The padlock ≠ honest.

HTTPS only means the connection is encrypted — scammers get free certificates too. A padlock on a fake login page still shows a padlock. Check the name, not the lock.

05

When in doubt, go direct.

Never use the link from the message. Type the address yourself or use the official app. If the warning was real, it will be waiting inside your account.
